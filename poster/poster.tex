\firstsection{Why it matters}
Schedulers treat \emph{who may see the data} and \emph{what a placement costs in energy} as separate problems.
\begin{itemize}
  \item AI's energy and environmental footprint is large and under-reported [7,\,8,\,9].
  \item Protected execution costs up to ≈\,7\,\% throughput for LLM inference on confidential H100 GPUs [6], and confidential GPUs are scarce.
  \item To our knowledge, energy-aware schedulers [1,\,2,\,4] do not treat security eligibility as a constraint.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contribution}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \seasname{}: eligibility first, then energy vs response time; every decision explained.
  \item A preregistered, reproducible benchmark with ablations.
  \item Evidence, in this simulated setting, of where placement-only energy optimisation stops paying off.
\end{itemize}

\section{Question \& hypotheses}
If security eligibility is a hard constraint, how much \emph{modelled} energy can placement still save without missing more deadlines?\par\vspace{4mm}
{\capsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{13mm}p{\dimexpr\colA-17mm}@{}}
\textbf{\color{navy}H1} & \seasname{} uses less dynamic energy than EFT \emph{and} is non-inferior on misses (margin \HOneNIMargin\,pp).\\[2mm]
\textbf{\color{navy}H2} & The gain grows with the share of security-constrained jobs.\\[2mm]
\textbf{\color{navy}H3} & The energy gain vs EFT and fewer misses than energy-greedy survive noisy estimates ($\sigma=25\,\%$).\\
\end{tabular}\par\vspace{3mm}
\color{muted}Preregistered; paired tests; Holm correction.\par}

\section{How \seasname{} decides}
A node is \emph{eligible} only if it offers every required capability and enough memory:
\[ S_{jn}=\mathbb{1}\!\left[R_j\subseteq C_n,\ m_j\le M_n\right] \]
Among eligible nodes that still meet the deadline, \seasname{} picks
\[ n^{*}=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{n}\ \alpha\,\hat E_{jn}+(1-\alpha)\,\hat R_{jn} \]
($\hat E$ energy, $\hat R$ response time, both relative to the best option). Least-slack first, online, non-preemptive; a greedy heuristic.

\firstsection{Limits of placement alone}
\figfile{figE_energy_budget}
\caption{Exploratory. Where \seasname{}'s modelled energy goes (S1 mean; always-on nodes over the service window). Floor: relaxation bound, each completed job on its cheapest eligible node.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Placement only moves dynamic energy, and \seasname{} is within \GapSeas\,\% of its floor (EFT: \GapEft\,\%).
  \item Under ideal power gating the energy saving disappears (total \TotGatedRel\,\%, CI [\TotGatedLo, \TotGatedHi]; exploratory).
  \item Robust to noisy estimates, $\sigma=25\,\%$ (H3 supported, Holm $p=\HThreePHolm$). The weight $\alpha$ barely matters (exploratory): identical schedules for $\alpha\ge0.25$ in \AlphaSameSeeds{} of \AlphaNSeeds{} seeds.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{2mm}
{\bfseries\color{navy}Negative results set the agenda:} the remaining energy is in \emph{when nodes are on}, and in whether the model matches hardware.
